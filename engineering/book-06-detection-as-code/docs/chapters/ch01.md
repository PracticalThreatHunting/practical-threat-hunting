# Chapter 1: The Detection That Could Not Ship

This is a cumulative study checkpoint in the edition, not a separate historical release.

## Artifact and proof scope

Case: a promising hunt finding stalls because nobody defined its consumer, owner, response expectation, acceptance criteria, or priority.

Build: detections/DET-001/intake/request.yaml; detections/DET-001/intake/stakeholders.yaml; definitions of ready and done; primary and backup owner; intended analyst-facing outcome; non-goals; priority rubric; initial lifecycle record.

Prove: schema validation; missing-owner and missing-acceptance-criterion negative tests; review checklist.

Boundary: the detection opportunity is supplied. Spend no more than a brief paragraph on the threat rationale; do not reteach hunting.

## Exercises and answer criteria

1. Remove the owner from a disposable copy of the DET-001 contract and confirm that policy validation rejects it. Explain why a backup owner does not satisfy a missing primary-owner field in this model.
2. Write two acceptance criteria for the signal-review consumer: one about evidence and one about duplicate delivery. Make each criterion falsifiable with a small fixture or interface test. Avoid “useful,” “fast,” and “high quality” unless you define the observation behind the adjective.
3. Compare an observable, lower-priority product with a high-priority product whose required source is absent. Propose a sequencing decision that records both value and readiness, identifies the missing dependency owner, and specifies the evidence that would change the decision.

Acceptance names the consumer, independent primary/backup ownership, and an executable positive and negative condition. Missing authority must fail readiness.
